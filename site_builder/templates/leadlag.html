Do developer-adoption signals lead revenue?

A research note on six public data-infrastructure companies: Snowflake, Datadog, MongoDB, Confluent, Elastic and Cloudflare.

1. Question

Analysts increasingly use alternative data such as package downloads and job postings to read product adoption ahead of reported numbers. If developers adopting a data-infrastructure product shows up first in what they install and what employers hire for, then growth in those signals should precede growth in reported revenue. This note tests that directly: does year-over-year growth in a company's developer-adoption signals correlate with its year-over-year revenue growth 0–4 quarters later?

2. Data

{{ data_table | safe }}

Revenue: SEC XBRL company facts (10-K/10-Q). Q4 is derived as full-year minus nine-month YTD. Fiscal years ending in January (SNOW, MDB) and April (ESTC) are handled by aggregating every signal over each company's exact fiscal-quarter dates. HN share: share of monthly Who is hiring? job posts mentioning the product, excluding the company's own job posts and customer/investor lists. npm: the company's official client package (proxy rationale). PyPI history only began ~180 days before the first pull, so PyPI cannot be tested yet.

3. Method

  • Variables: log YoY growth (same fiscal quarter a year earlier) of revenue and of each signal.
  • Lags: corr(signalt−k, revenuet) for k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 quarters. All lags are reported; none is selected.
  • Uncertainty: YoY series are highly autocorrelated, so 30 quarters are worth far fewer independent observations. CIs and p-values use a Bartlett effective sample size (neff). A null simulation run before any data was pulled showed that a naive test flags up to 58% of unrelated persistent series as “significant”; the neff test keeps that near 5%.
  • Multiple testing: a Bonferroni p over the five lags (paying for “picking the best lag”) and Benjamini-Hochberg q-values across all company tests.
  • Pooled panel: the six companies stacked with company fixed effects, and with company + calendar-quarter fixed effects. The second version removes anything common to all six in a quarter (rates, the 2021 boom and 2022–23 cost-cutting, registry-wide download inflation).
  • Pre-registration: specifications, lags and verdict rules were committed to the repo before the first data pull. Two signals were added after seeing the data and are labelled post-hoc: npm downloads relative to a basket of mature packages, added because every npm package's downloads inflated together in 2026. Post-hoc results are kept in a separate multiple-testing family.

5. What this can and can't tell you

Can
  • Whether, over the available history, faster growth in a developer signal was followed by faster reported revenue growth, with honest uncertainty bands.
  • Whether any apparent relationship survives autocorrelation, the five lags tried, and a common cycle hitting all six companies at once.
Can't
  • Prove causation or forecast revenue. A shared driver (rates, the 2021 boom, 2022–23 cost-cutting) can move both.
  • Detect small effects. Each company has roughly 15–35 quarterly YoY points, and an effective sample often under 10. A null result here means “not detectable with this data”, not “no relationship”.
  • Isolate paid usage. Proxies include open-source and free-tier use (community MongoDB, Apache Kafka, OpenSearch users of Elastic clients) and CI or bot downloads. HN skews toward US startups.
  • Use PyPI yet. pypistats keeps only ~180 days; PyPI YoY becomes testable after about a year of weekly appends.

This is a research exercise, not investment advice.
